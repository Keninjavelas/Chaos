# Sector 5: Communications Office

**Purpose:** Contact
**Dimensions:** 8m x 10m
**Location:** Back of Records Hall (`[-15, 0, -15]`)
**Atmosphere:** Operational, isolating, humming with electricity. Deep blue-green monitor glow. It feels like the last functioning part of an otherwise dead facility.

---

## Design Philosophy
Most portfolios fail at the "Contact" page. It usually devolves into a generic list of standard utility links: Email, LinkedIn, GitHub, Resume. 

In The Archive, the Communications Office functions as an **Emergency Network Operations Center (NOC)**. It transforms the utilitarian act of contacting the developer into a narrative discovery. The psychological horror in this room isn't decay—it is isolation. The machines are still desperately trying to broadcast, but nobody is answering.

---

## Visual Identity
Upon entering the room, the dim yellow fluorescent lighting of the facility is replaced by the stark, sterile blue-green glow of server racks and CRT monitors. 

### Left Wall: The Server Racks
A row of towering server racks hums loudly. Blinking green and red LEDs line the blades. One specific server projects a floating red warning: `CRITICAL: DRIVE FAILURE DETECTED`.

### Right Wall: The Operator Stations
Three operator desks face the wall, physically demonstrating the facility's decline.
- **Desk 1:** Dead. The monitors are static gray, the keyboard is covered in dust, and an abandoned coffee mug sits nearby.
- **Desk 2:** Frozen. A terminal screen displays a fatal error: `CONNECTION TIMED OUT`.
- **Desk 3:** The only active terminal in the room. Beside the glowing monitors sits an empty photo frame—a small, highly personal reminder that this facility was once occupied by a real person.

Behind Desk 3 is a massive **Network Topology Map**. It lists the facility's connection nodes (Reception -> Personnel -> Records -> Communications) but ends with an `UNKNOWN NODE` aggressively circled in red marker, foreshadowing the descent into Sublevel -1.

---

## The Core Interactions

### 1. The External Relay (Desk 3)
Instead of a simple "Links" page, Desk 3 houses three glowing CRT monitors that serve as the External Relay. Interacting with them opens the UI with massive, clickable terminal buttons:
- **Monitor 1 (GitHub):** "ARCHIVE REPOSITORY INDEX. Repositories Located: 34. [ ACCESS REPOSITORIES ]"
- **Monitor 2 (LinkedIn):** "Professional Network Relay. [ OPEN PROFESSIONAL NETWORK ]"
- **Monitor 3 (Email):** "Direct Communication Channel. [ SEND TRANSMISSION ]"

### 2. Emergency Resume Archive
Mounted to the back wall is a red, fireproof emergency cabinet. The glass is cracked, and a red warning light flashes above it. 
A label underneath reads: **"BREAK GLASS IN CASE OF RECRUITER"**.
Interacting with it adds the `Resume.pdf` file directly into the player's inventory.

### 3. The Outbound Message Queue
Near the entrance, a small terminal quietly humanizes the struggle of early career engineers by reframing the job hunt as a series of failing transmissions:
- `Outbound Transmission #138: No Response`
- `Outbound Transmission #142: No Response`
- `Outbound Transmission #156: Delivery Failed`
- `Outbound Transmission #187: Awaiting Acknowledgement`

---

## Environmental Storytelling
The room's lore confirms the facility's isolation. Scattered papers document the timeline of signal loss:
- **Log A:** "No response received."
- **Log B:** "Retrying transmission."
- **Log C:** "Connection established. Date: [REMOVED]"

For explorers, an **Audio Tape** is hidden behind the massive server racks on the left wall. The transcript is simple: *"If you're hearing this, the systems still work. That's enough."*

---

## Room Progression
Without ever reading a traditional contact page, the recruiter discovers the developer's GitHub, LinkedIn, Email, and Resume naturally. 

- **Personnel Wing:** This is who he is.
- **Records Hall:** This is what he can build.
- **Communications Office:** This is how you reach him.
- **Sublevel -1:** This is what happens when curiosity wins.
