# Sector 2: Records Hall

**Purpose:** Capability
**Dimensions:** 14m x 18m
**Location:** Left Corridor `[-15, 0, 0]`
**Atmosphere:** Cold, clinical, vast. Fluorescent lighting flickers slightly. The room is messy, littered with debris and documentation, suggesting an investigation that was abandoned in a hurry.

---

## Design Philosophy
The Records Hall is the facility's largest room and the core of the portfolio experience. Unlike standard portfolios that list "Projects," the Records Hall presents evidence of **engineering domains**.

It shifts the psychological framing from *"Look at my work"* to *"Look at what happened here."* Recruiters uncover capability through investigation.

The room is divided into three physical and narrative tiers:
1. **The Public Archive** (The Hook)
2. **The Active Investigation Table** (The Core)
3. **The Restricted Archive** (The Trajectory)

---

## 1. The Public Archive (The Hook)
Lining the walls near the entrance are four massive filing cabinets representing the core engineering domains. Opening them does not reveal the full project, only the Metadata.

1. **Cabinet 12:** Deployment Records (AWS Static Website)
2. **Cabinet 21:** Containment Reports (Research Authorization Dossier)
3. **Cabinet 33:** Operations Archive (Engineering Incident Archive)
4. **Cabinet 47:** Restricted Programs (Experimental Systems)

*This tier builds curiosity. The recruiter discovers the domains (AWS, Security, DevOps, Kubernetes) but it feels like a facility archive rather than a portfolio showcase.*

---

## 2. The Active Investigation Table (The Core)
The focal point of the room. A massive, clustered set of desks pushed together in the center of the hall, covered in scattered documentation and incident reports.

This is where the recruiter finds the **Current Investigation**. It is not a list of past achievements; it is the project you are actively obsessed with. 

### File A: Current Major Project (Platform Laboratory)
* Focus: Enterprise-grade observability and automated deployment.
* Content: True systems engineering requires chaotic, stateful environments to debug.
* **The Evidence Locker**: Contains the Repository link, Network Topology, and Load Test Metrics.

### The Ceiling-Hung Archive Map
Directly above the investigation table hangs a massive, yellowed facility map suspended by chains. It serves as the room's iconic visual centerpiece. 
The map shows the facility layout, with certain areas aggressively crossed out in red marker ("DO NOT ACCESS", "DATA REMOVED"). It grounds the player in the horror narrative while physically anchoring the massive empty vertical space above the table.

---

## 3. The Restricted Archive (The Trajectory)
The back 6 meters of the room are fenced off by a massive chain-link security gate with a broken lock. It represents ambition and future work rather than completed projects.

1. **Kubernetes Initiative** (Ongoing)
2. **Open Source Contribution Program** (Pending Deployment)
3. **Distributed Systems Lab** (Research Phase)

*Recruiters love seeing trajectory. Hiding these behind a security gate frames the "unfinished" nature of the work as highly classified/experimental rather than incomplete.*

---

## Environmental Storytelling & Contradictions
To ensure the Records Hall retains its psychological horror framing, the facility's documentation is deliberately unreliable. Scattered around the investigation table and floor are three separate copies of **Incident Report 14**.
1. The first claims the incident is "Resolved".
2. The second claims it is "Escalating" and the power must be pulled.
3. The third claims it "Never Occurred" because the servers do not physically exist.

This subtle contradiction reinforces the investigation theme—asking the recruiter to piece together a fragmented narrative.
By the time a recruiter or engineer leaves the Records Hall, they should have absorbed the following:
* **The Stack:** Docker, Kubernetes, AWS, Terraform, Go, Rust.
* **The Domains:** Cloud, Security, Infrastructure, Platform Engineering.
* **The Conclusion:** This person builds complex backend systems, not just simple frontend applications.
